What a Gamer Needs in Nigeria: Phone, Tablet, Controller & Audio (2026)

By Mikee Gadget Plug • Updated November 26, 2026 • 6 min read

Short answer

For mobile gaming you need a phone with a flagship chip and a 120Hz screen, at least 256GB storage, a controller if you play shooters or football games, low-latency audio, a stable internet connection with low ping, and a power bank. The iPhone 18 Pro and Galaxy S26 Ultra are the strongest gaming phones we sell; a UK used iPhone 14 Pro or Galaxy S23 is great on a budget.

Key takeaways

  • Chip and screen refresh rate matter more than RAM for smooth gaming.
  • Big games (COD Mobile, Genshin, eFootball) take 10–30GB each, so get 256GB or more.
  • Ping matters more than download speed. Test your network at night when you play.
  • A controller and wired or low-latency earphones give you a real advantage.

1. The gaming phone

What actually makes a phone good for gaming:

  • Chip: a recent flagship chip (Apple A-series Pro, Snapdragon 8 Elite) keeps frame rates high without lag.
  • 120Hz screen: makes everything look and feel smoother, and helps your aim in shooters.
  • Cooling and battery: long sessions heat phones up and drain batteries; bigger phones handle both better.
  • Storage: 256GB minimum for a few big games plus your photos.

2. Tablet: the bigger screen advantage

A tablet shows more of the map and makes aiming easier. iPads are especially popular because most mobile games are well optimised for them.

  • iPad Air 11" M3 (from ₦899,000): the sweet spot for gaming.
  • iPad Pro 11" (from ₦1,949,000): 120Hz ProMotion screen for competitive play.
  • iPad mini (from ₦1,349,000): light, easy to hold for long sessions, a favourite for PUBG.

3. Controller

For eFootball, EA FC Mobile, COD Mobile and emulators, a controller makes a big difference. A PlayStation 5 DualSense or Xbox controller connects by Bluetooth to iPhones, iPads and most Android phones. Add a phone clip to hold your phone above the controller.

4. Audio: hear footsteps first

In shooters, sound tells you where enemies are. Bluetooth earbuds add delay (latency), so for competitive play:

  • Use wired earphones through a USB-C adapter for zero delay, or
  • Pair earbuds with the same brand's phone, which usually gives lower delay: Galaxy Buds 3 Pro (from ₦220,000) with a Samsung (turn on Game Mode), or AirPods Pro 3 (from ₦330,000) with an iPhone.
  • For long home sessions, over-ear headphones like the JBL Tune 720BT (from ₦80,000) are comfortable and affordable.

5. Internet: ping beats speed

Online games use little data but need a fast, steady response (low ping). 20–60ms is good; above 100ms you'll feel lag.

  • Test your network at the time you play; evening congestion is real.
  • Keep a second SIM on another network for when your main one lags.
  • At home, play close to the router or use a 5GHz WiFi connection.

6. Power

Gaming drains batteries fast. Get a 20,000mAh power bank with fast charging, and use a quality charger; cheap chargers heat phones up and damage batteries over time.

7. Console and PC gamers

If you play on PS5, Xbox or PC, the same rules apply: wired ethernet or a strong 5GHz connection, a UPS or inverter so a power cut doesn't end your match, and a 120Hz TV or monitor with HDMI 2.1 to get the most from current consoles.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best gaming phone in Nigeria?

At the top end, the iPhone 18 Pro Max and Samsung Galaxy S26 Ultra. For value, a UK used iPhone 14 Pro or Galaxy S23 gives smooth gameplay for much less.

How much storage do I need for gaming?

At least 256GB. Big games like COD Mobile, eFootball and Genshin Impact can take 10–30GB each, and they grow with updates.

Is iPhone or Android better for gaming?

Both are excellent at the top end. iPhones get very stable performance and most games are well optimised for them; Android gives more choice, emulators and customisation.

Can I use a PS5 controller with my phone?

Yes. A PS5 DualSense controller pairs by Bluetooth with iPhones, iPads and most Android phones.
